01The published-case range

Each data protection finding (ICO) has a type, such as consent missing or suppression breach. For each type we hold a range of what comparable organisations were fined in published ICO decisions, split by annual turnover. We call this the frame. Your figure can never go outside it.

02Factors that move the figure

Where you sit inside the frame depends on aggravating factors (push it up) and mitigating factors (pull it down), the same things a regulator weighs. Every factor comes from a fixed list with a fixed weight. They come from three places:

  • Profile  Your business profile. Turnover, list size, opt-in records, complaints and regulator contact. This is why the profile is required.
  • Rule  Facts we already hold about the finding. Contacts affected, data types, DPA status, whether a documented assessment exists, known breach history, and how long the finding has been open.
  • AI  An AI reading of the finding, only if you ask for one. It can only tick factors from the AI part of this list, and each must come with a quote of at least three words that appears word for word in the finding. Anything else is thrown away, never repaired. The reading is saved and reused until the evidence changes.
Loading…

Contacts affected comes from the finding itself first, then a number the AI quoted from it, then the list size on your profile. Opt-in record factors apply to consent and opt-out findings only. Turnover counts as the lower or upper part of its band when it sits in the bottom or top third (measured on a log scale). If your profile confirms complaints, the AI’s reading of complaints is ignored so it is never counted twice.

05Adding findings together

Adding every finding’s figure together would overstate things: a regulator investigates related failings as one case and issues one penalty. So findings are grouped by area, and each area is counted once.

Loading…
Loading…

Next to the total we show an attention likelihood (Low, Medium or High). It is never multiplied into the figure. It adds points for: critical findings open (+2), the largest audience affected (+2 over 100,000, +1 over 10,000), an issue type the ICO has fined recently such as opt-outs or bought-in data (+2), a finance, health or gambling sector (+1), and findings open for more than 90 days (+1). Five points or more is High, three or more is Medium.

06Advertising (ASA) and consumer law (CMA)

ASA: no fines, so no fine figure

The ASA does not fine. An upheld ruling is published permanently under your brand name, and serious or repeat cases can be referred to Trading Standards. So ASA findings show a referral risk, never a fine.

Loading…

CMA: the statutory maximum only

Since the DMCCA 2024 the CMA can fine directly. There are not yet enough published decisions to anchor a realistic range, so we show only the legal maximum for a business your size, worked out from the top of your turnover band.

Loading…

Commercial cost (your own figures, never a fine)

If you give a campaign’s budget and expected sales, we estimate what an ASA or CMA finding could cost the business. These figures are labelled as business cost and are never added to regulatory exposure.

Risk addressed

The exposure removed by the fixes you have completed: your total with those findings still open, minus your total now, using the grouping in step 5. It is split into fixes verified by re-running the check and fixes you marked fixed yourself.

Already lost

A commercial figure, not a regulatory one: extra unsubscribes over the last 12 months (from Audience Read) multiplied by your cost per subscriber. If you have not entered a cost per subscriber we use a sector default and label the figure as an estimate.
